# Wakeel Export — Preliminary Feasibility & Impact Assessment

**Research cut-off:** 19 November 2026  
**Stage:** Proposal / prototype. No operating history, verified transactions, grant awards or measured impacts are claimed.  
**Status:** Preliminary evidence-led desk assessment, not a completed comprehensive feasibility study, bankable business plan, statutory environmental assessment, legal opinion or government-approved appraisal. Primary buyer/supplier research, product-market sizing, local legal opinion and cost quotations remain outstanding.
**Current scope:** research, documentation and decision presentation only. No operating launch, hiring, contracting, funding request or grant application is proposed at this stage.

## 1. Executive assessment

Wakeel proposes a digital trade-enablement model connecting Egyptian manufacturers with international buyer demand through independent marketers, while licensed export entities retain regulated export execution and authorised financial institutions handle settlement. Its hypothesis is that reducing buyer-discovery and coordination costs can help convert more international demand into viable Egyptian export orders.

**Preliminary conclusion:** the concept has strategic and development rationale, but feasibility remains conditional on verified buyer demand and conversion; a legally workable marketer attribution/compensation model; committed licensed exporter, bank and logistics partners; differentiated supplier capability and viable transaction economics; and a funded pilot with auditable baselines and safeguards.

The available evidence does not establish Wakeel's obtainable market share, acquisition cost, conversion rate, incremental exports or break-even point. These require primary validation. No five-year forecast is fabricated.

| Dimension | Preliminary view | Evidence required |
|---|---|---|
| Strategic | Positive alignment with export diversification, SME access and trade digitalisation | Formal sponsor and scope |
| Market / commercial | Plausible, unproven | Verified RFQs, buyer interviews, supplier quotations, conversion funnel |
| Technical | Prototype only | Working workflow, data governance and integration tests |
| Legal / regulatory | Conditional; material gate | Egyptian legal opinion, regulator engagement and written partner approvals |
| Financial | Not yet bankable | EGP quotations, five-year model, cash flow, unit economics and tax review |
| Economic / social | Potentially material, not attributable yet | Baseline, counterfactual and independent evaluation |

**Recommendation:** consider only a bounded institutional review of the proposal. Any later market validation, legal review or detailed appraisal should occur only if the competent decision-maker accepts the concept for further study. No launch or national rollout is recommended on the present evidence.

## 2. Operating model and theory of change

Manufacturers provide accurate product, capacity, price, quality and compliance information. Independent marketers identify buyers, register leads and support follow-up within approved boundaries. A licensed export company executes export and issues export documents. Banks and authorised institutions handle regulated receipt and settlement. Freight forwarders, customs brokers and carriers perform their authorised services. The platform records opportunities, attribution, status and evidence.

The marketer is not the legal exporter, customs broker, payment service provider or collector of goods-sale proceeds. This separation must be validated against actual contracts, payment flows and Egyptian law.

**Inputs:** platform, verified suppliers, licensed export partners, bank/logistics partners, market intelligence, trained marketers and compliance framework.  
**Activities:** qualify buyer demand, match supplier capability, support introductions, coordinate licensed execution and train participants.  
**Outputs:** verified supplier profiles, qualified buyers, matched opportunities, quotations, completed transactions and market-gap reports.  
**Potential outcomes:** lower discovery costs, more qualified leads, better supplier-market fit, wider participation and better information on unmet demand.  
**Long-term impacts (not guaranteed):** additional exports and FX receipts, stronger SME competitiveness, diversified products/markets, additional income and resource-efficient value chains.

## 3. Evidence base: facts versus targets

### Official national export baseline
The Ministry of Investment and Foreign Trade / GOEIC reported Egyptian non-oil exports of **USD 48.567bn in 2025**, compared with USD 41.507bn in 2024, approximately 17% growth. Leading 2025 sectors included building materials (USD 14.880bn), chemicals and fertilisers (USD 9.419bn), food industries (USD 6.803bn), engineering and electronics (USD 6.468bn), and agricultural products (USD 4.692bn). These are national historical data, not Wakeel sales or an addressable market estimate.

Source: [SIS — 2025 foreign trade indicators, 27 January 2026](https://sis.gov.eg/en/media-center/news/investment-minister-reviews-2025-foreign-trade-indicators/).

The proposed 15% increase is an ambitious target/scenario, not a government forecast or an established Wakeel contribution.

CAPMAS-reported 2025 data published by SIS show unemployment of 6.3%, youth unemployment (ages 15–29) of 13.2%, and female unemployment of 22.5% in urban areas and 8.8% in rural areas. ## 4. Economic and commercial feasibility

### Economic rationale
Potential value arises from converting previously missed or poorly served demand into viable Egyptian supply contracts. The platform may reduce search, matching and coordination costs, improve visibility of smaller suppliers and reveal product gaps. Potential benefits include additional orders and FX receipts, supplier capacity utilisation, improved specifications and quality, diversification, lower discovery costs and evidence for industrial investment.

Potential costs include platform development/maintenance, market intelligence, onboarding, verification, training, legal/compliance, partner coordination, customer acquisition, complaints, working capital, payment delays and FX exposure.

### Demand intelligence and market sizing
A defensible model must be product-by-product and destination-by-destination. National export totals are not TAM or Wakeel's expected sales.

1. Use HS-6 product codes and validate definitions.
2. Extract five-year import values, volumes, unit values and growth by destination from [ITC Trade Map](https://www.trademap.org/) and [UN Comtrade](https://comtradeplus.un.org/).
3. Screen with [ITC Export Potential Map](https://exportpotential.intracen.org/), noting its current export-potential estimate is for 2030 and model-based.
4. Check tariffs, rules of origin and non-tariff measures through [ITC Market Access Map](https://www.macmap.org/) and [EU Access2Markets](https://trade.ec.europa.eu/access-to-markets/en/home).
5. Collect real RFQs, tenders and buyer specifications. Commercial directories such as Europages are lead sources, not proof of demand or buyer solvency.
6. Interview buyers and suppliers; validate price, MOQ, certifications, lead time, packaging, payment terms and Incoterms.
7. Match only suppliers with verified capacity and export-document readiness; deduplicate buyer/RFQ records and retain source/date evidence.

Methodology: [ITC Export Potential Map data sources](https://exportpotential.intracen.org/resources/data-sources).

### TAM / SAM / SOM
- **TAM:** annual import demand for selected HS-6 products in relevant destinations.
- **SAM:** portion Egyptian suppliers can serve at required quality, certification, price, volume, logistics and market-access conditions.
- **SOM:** portion realistically convertible given verified suppliers, buyer pipeline, sales-cycle length, conversion, partner capacity and support capacity.

Bottom-up cell formula: qualified annual buyer demand × realistic supplier fulfilment share × expected realised price.  
SOM formula: qualified opportunities × observed win rate × average realised order value, capped by supplier and licensed execution capacity.

Do not apply a generic share to Egypt's total exports/imports or count one order more than once.

### Revenue and unit economics
The proposal is 0.6% total: 0.5% marketer and 0.1% platform. Contract terms must define eligible collected value, exclusions, currency conversion, partial payments, returns, cancellations, tax treatment, attribution and payment timing.

- Platform gross fee = eligible collected transaction value × 0.001.
- Marketer fee = eligible collected transaction value × 0.005.

No fee is earned on registration, lead submission, quotation or an uncollected order. Measure acquisition cost per verified/active marketer, cost per qualified buyer/RFQ, RFQ-to-quote and quote-to-order conversion, average order value, collected platform fee, variable cost per transaction, disputes, cancellations and contribution margin.

### Five-year financial model — evidence gate
A defensible five-year forecast cannot be populated from this concept alone: historic funnel, average order value, win rate, collections, retention, acquisition cost, tax profile and partner prices are not evidenced. Inventing these would create false precision.

Build in EGP, with monthly year-one detail and annual years two–five. Include low/base/high demand and conversion, staged hiring, written quotations, employer social insurance and taxes, working capital/payment delays, income statement, cash flow, break-even and sensitivities. Separate platform revenue, government execution contract and restricted grants.

Break-even = annual fixed costs ÷ contribution margin per completed transaction. Grants are not recurring commercial revenue; gross merchandise value is not platform revenue.

### Pilot budget
The existing USD 229.2k–283.2k estimate and suggested USD 300k-equivalent ceiling are preliminary, not validated Egyptian quotations, contract price or approved public budget. Rebuild in EGP with comparable quotations, tax/social-insurance review, staged hiring and contingency. Test whether all 18 roles are required at launch.

## 5. Social feasibility and inclusion

The concept could widen access to export-related work without making participants legal exporters. It may offer flexible participation for women, youth, people outside major commercial centres and people with disabilities, and connect SMEs, cooperatives and local producers to buyers. These are potential benefits, not measured outcomes. Registration is not employment; active participation requires evidenced activity and income requires verified payment.

Safeguards: disclose commission conditions and risks; no guaranteed-income claims or hidden fees; transparent attribution and disputes; accessible Arabic-first training; non-discrimination and safe complaints; data minimisation and privacy; do not count unpaid family work as paid employment; monitor drop-off, delayed payments and adverse effects.

Measure unique registrations, verified participants, active marketers, recipients of paid income, median net income, lawful disaggregation by gender/age/governorate, retention, complaints/resolution and pre/post skills gains.

## 6. Cultural feasibility and heritage safeguards

Wakeel could create routes to buyers for Egyptian crafts, design, food traditions and creative products, supporting artisans and intergenerational skills. Risks include appropriation, imitation, loss of community control, misleading origin claims, design homogenisation and unfair value capture.

Require informed permission for community names/images/stories/symbols/traditional knowledge; verified maker and origin; respect for copyright, trademarks, collective marks and geographical indications; fair pricing, attribution and benefit-sharing; community review for sensitive products; traceability from artisan to buyer.

Measure artisan orders and net income, active craft producers, repeat orders, skills transfer, consent compliance, attribution accuracy and producer control—not merely product listings.

## 7. Environmental and climate feasibility

LCL consolidation may improve container utilisation and reduce per-unit freight emissions only where it replaces less efficient alternatives. Consolidation can also add handling, warehousing or detours. Benefits must be measured against a counterfactual.

For each shipment record weight/volume, mode, route, distance, carrier, load factor, consolidation status, alternative shipment, emissions method, packaging mass, damage and returns. Indicators: kgCO2e per tonne-km and delivered unit; container utilisation; consolidated share; packaging mass/reuse; damage/returns; supplier energy/water improvements where material.

Use a recognised freight-accounting method and disclose boundaries, factors, data quality and uncertainty. Do not claim carbon neutrality, avoided emissions or green shipping without verified calculations. Screen products for hazardous materials, protected species, illegal timber/wildlife, restricted chemicals, waste-shipment rules, permits and destination restrictions.

## 8. International and regional feasibility

The project could support Egyptian participation in regional/global value chains through supplier discovery, buyer intelligence and trade-document readiness. Choose destinations based on product demand, tariffs, non-tariff requirements, freight economics, payment risk and supplier capacity.

Egypt's association with **Horizon Europe** from 22 October 2025 creates a research and innovation route. Eligible Egyptian entities can participate as beneficiaries under applicable calls; this does not make every commercial platform or call automatically eligible.

Source: [European Commission — Egypt and Horizon Europe](https://research-and-innovation.ec.europa.eu/strategy/strategy-research-and-innovation/europe-world/international-cooperation/association-horizon-europe/egypt_en).

Potential alignment—not endorsement—includes UN SDGs 8, 9, 10, 12, 13 and 17; AfCFTA; EU–Egypt cooperation on digital transformation, research, youth and culture; and buyer requirements on safety, traceability, due diligence, sustainability, labour and conformity.

## 9. Legal and regulatory feasibility

Before live transactions: obtain written Egyptian legal advice on marketer status, commission, agency/brokerage boundaries, tax/invoicing and employment classification; confirm exporter of record and buyer-contract ownership; confirm bank-approved FX receipt/settlement, KYC/AML, sanctions screening and permitted payouts; confirm customs, product, origin, export-control and destination requirements; sign supplier/buyer-introduction/attribution/data/dispute/commission agreements; define data roles, lawful basis, privacy and retention; obtain partner approvals and sandbox permissions where required; maintain auditable transaction records.

The 0.5% / 0.1% split and any tax relief/incentive remain proposals until approved and contracted. No tax exemption is assumed.

## 11. Pilot and decision gates

The proposed 180 days is a planning horizon, not a completion promise. Start only after approvals, contracts, funding and partner readiness.

- **Gate 0:** legal opinion; approved roles/contracts/fee terms; licensed exporter, bank and logistics partners; supplier verification; privacy/KYC/complaint/audit procedures.
- **Gate 1:** product-market selection using HS-6 data; verified buyer interviews/RFQs; price, quantity, certification and delivery confirmed; supplier capacity matched.
- **Gate 2:** limited cohort and transaction scope; unique ID/source evidence; reconcile shipment, collection, settlement and commission.
- **Gate 3:** baseline and comparison where feasible; conservative incremental-export and net-benefit estimate; income distribution and environmental measures; independent evaluation and stop/redesign/scale decision.

Previously proposed ranges (50–100 factories, 5,000–10,000 registered marketers, 500–1,000 active marketers, 100–200 buyers and 10–20 transactions) are draft targets only. Reset after demand and capacity testing.

## 12. Conclusion

**Current status: strategically plausible but not proven feasible or bankable.** The next research phase, if authorised by the competent decision-maker, should validate buyer/supplier demand in selected product-market cells, calculate bottom-up TAM/SAM/SOM, obtain specialist legal analysis, rebuild the EGP financial model from verified inputs, and establish social/cultural/environmental baselines. A controlled pilot is a separate future decision, not part of the present request.

Do not make national-scale commitments, guaranteed-income claims, export attribution, carbon claims or grant-funded operating-budget claims before evidence exists.
