## 1. Executive funding strategy

Wakeel should not submit one oversized request asking a single donor to finance every component. Its impact model spans trade facilitation, digital inclusion, employment, women’s economic empowerment, industrial innovation, cultural heritage, local livelihoods, climate action and biodiversity. These themes align with different funding instruments, applicant types and rules.

The recommended approach is a **modular blended-finance portfolio**:

1. **Commercial venture capital / catalytic co-financing** for the revenue-generating platform company and its local growth, where the programme expressly accepts for-profit start-ups.
2. **Non-profit grants** for separately governed public-benefit work packages: digital skills, women and youth inclusion, artisan market access, community livelihoods, environmental outcomes, research and independent impact measurement.
3. **Research and innovation grants** for universities and industrial partners developing new products, processes, standards or technologies in response to verified global demand.
4. **Concessional finance and risk-sharing** for eligible women-led SMEs, manufacturers, green investments and exporters through partner banks or financial institutions.
5. **Country-led / multilateral climate and heritage support** for activities that must be requested by the State Party or delivered through accredited entities and approved national mechanisms.

The funding portfolio can be designed to target **zero direct public-budget appropriation for incremental pilot costs**, subject to donor awards and signed agreements. It must not be described as already funded or as eliminating all public-sector economic costs. Regulatory staff time, in-kind contributions, facilities and oversight should be disclosed transparently.

### Core eligibility rule

A grant is awarded to an eligible legal applicant for eligible activities; it is not automatically awarded to the project idea or its founder. Many international grant programmes exclude for-profit applicants, require an NGO/university/public institution, require a European or German lead applicant, or fund end beneficiaries rather than the platform operator.

Wakeel should therefore use a genuine consortium with clear roles, not a nominal or shell NGO:
- Wakeel / the platform company: product and technology delivery only where the grant rules and procurement terms allow it.
- Egyptian NGO / cooperative / community organisation: local inclusion, livelihoods, artisan and community work packages.
- University / research centre: industrial demand analysis, product innovation, standards and evaluation.
- Licensed export company / manufacturer / business association: export execution, market access and industry engagement.
- Public authority: regulatory coordination and oversight under the applicable approval framework.
- International lead partner / accredited entity: where the programme requires it.

All parties, ownership of outputs, intellectual property, data access, procurement, conflicts of interest and audit responsibilities must be written into the partnership and grant agreements.

### Immediate qualification notes

**develoPPP Ventures:** This is the clearest current route for the commercial venture itself, but it requires proof of concept through initial revenue, a financial statement, and matching capital at least equal to the programme contribution. The matching capital must be secured by disbursement; the programme does not remove the need for private co-financing. The current call is specifically available in Egypt and closes 31 December 2026. [Official call and eligibility](https://www.developpp.de/en/application/ventures/).

**IKI Small Grants:** The 2026 call is particularly relevant to community-led environmental work and explicitly expects Indigenous Peoples and local communities (IPLCs) to be empowered and involved in design and implementation. It is not a general-purpose export-platform grant. The applicant and activities must be non-profit and directly contribute to nature-based solutions. [Official call](https://www.international-climate-initiative.com/en/find-funding/small-grants/small-grants-2026/).

**IKI Medium Grants:** The 2026 themes include green skills for local climate action and digital/practical solutions for resilient coasts and marine ecosystems. It is a consortium route with a Germany-based lead, not a direct application by an Egyptian for-profit platform. [Official call](https://www.international-climate-initiative.com/en/find-funding/medium-grants/medium-grants-2026).

**STDF / Egyptian-Spanish Call 11:** Suitable only if Wakeel identifies specific industrial demand gaps that can be addressed through research, product/process development or technology transfer with a qualifying research institution and industrial partners. [STDF calls](https://stdf.eg/web/grants/open).

### Funding portfolio rule

Do not place the same cost or activity in multiple grant budgets. Maintain one consolidated project budget with unique cost IDs, donor restrictions, matching-fund sources, procurement rules, audit trail, and a cost-allocation methodology. Any matching contribution must be genuine, eligible, documented and not double-counted.

## 5. Zero-direct-public-budget target: financial and legal safeguards

The funding proposition to the Egyptian Government should be phrased as:

> **Wakeel is designed to run its incremental pilot and public-benefit work packages without requiring a direct appropriation from the Egyptian State budget, by mobilising international grants, technical assistance, private-sector co-financing and development partnerships. Any government participation can be limited to regulatory oversight, approvals and transparently recorded in-kind coordination, subject to the competent authorities. No public expenditure is assumed unless expressly approved.**

This is a funding design objective, not a guarantee. It becomes credible only when the project has secured written donor commitments sufficient to cover the approved scope and cash-flow schedule.

### Reimbursement and bridge-finance rule

Some programmes pay advances, some reimburse eligible expenditure, and others pay against milestones or deliverables. Before relying on post-expenditure reimbursement:
- Obtain the signed grant agreement and confirm the payment schedule.
- Confirm in writing that each cost category, procurement method and contracting party is eligible.
- Check whether costs incurred before the agreement's effective date are excluded.
- Identify who will provide bridge liquidity if the grant reimburses after expenditure.
- Confirm currency, exchange-rate, tax, VAT, audit, retention and documentation rules.
- Do not commit government funds, supplier credit or founder capital on the assumption that a future grant will repay them.
- Do not describe a grant award as a government guarantee, sovereign guarantee or binding donor commitment unless the relevant instrument expressly says so.

### Funding evidence ladder for a government submission

| Evidence level | What can be claimed |
|---|---|
| Donor identified | Potential funding source only |
| Programme fit assessed | Thematic fit appears plausible; eligibility still unconfirmed |
| Written donor dialogue / expression of interest | Engagement is underway; no funding commitment |
| Eligible concept note submitted | Application submitted; award not assured |
| Shortlisted / invited to full proposal | Competitive progress only |
| Formal award letter with conditions | Conditional award; conditions must be satisfied |
| Signed grant agreement and effective date | Contractual funding commitment under its terms |
| First disbursement received | Funds received and available subject to restrictions |

For a credible government financing paper, use a portfolio of **secured, conditional and pipeline funding** with separate totals. Never aggregate unawarded opportunities as committed money.

## 6. Minimum donor-readiness package

Before submitting to major donors, prepare:
1. A two-page concept note and a 10–15 page full proposal, with a clear theory of change.
2. A work-breakdown structure separating commercial platform costs from public-benefit activities.
3. A results framework with baselines, targets, definitions, verification sources and reporting frequency.
4. A detailed budget by work package, cost category, year, partner and funding source.
5. A consortium map and signed letters of commitment from genuine eligible partners.
6. Legal identity, registration, governance, audited accounts, annual reports and financial-management procedures of the proposed applicant.
7. Procurement, conflict-of-interest, anti-fraud, safeguarding, data-protection, grievance and whistleblowing procedures proportionate to each donor's rules.
8. Gender action plan and inclusion measures for women, youth, persons with disabilities and under-served governorates.
9. Community engagement and benefit-sharing plan for craftspeople, cooperatives and local communities, including consent and traditional-knowledge safeguards.
10. Environmental and social screening, climate rationale and a defensible methodology for measuring emissions/resource efficiency.
11. IP, software licensing, data ownership, open standards and post-grant sustainability plan.
12. Cash-flow forecast distinguishing advance payments, milestone payments, reimbursement and matching finance.
